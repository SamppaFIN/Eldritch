Eldritch Sanctuary v3 · visual system

Sigil

A mobile-first visual system for a game played outdoors, one-handed, while walking. Three things carry it: true liquid glass as the only chrome material, a semantic colour law where every resource owns one hue everywhere it is ever named, and a procedural hex that says who owns the ground and what stands on it without a tap.

01

The colour law

A resource is never written in body ink. Wherever food is named — a number, an icon, a tech effect, a leaderboard row — it is food green. Gold is gold. This is the one rule that makes a dense screen scannable at walking pace.

02

True liquid glass

Five stacked layers, not one backdrop-filter. The pane refracts what is behind it, catches a specular edge along its top-left, and casts light back into the map. Text and icons sit on a separate opaque ink layer above the glass so contrast never depends on what the map is doing underneath.

◆
Sunlight clause. Glass is a chrome material. Text, numbers and icons render at full opacity on the ink layer above it, and every modal that must be read adds the deep tint (--glass-tint-deep, 0.74 α). Nothing that must be legible outdoors is ever alpha-faded onto the map.
Daylight mode · one switch, designed now

Direct sun is the one condition glass cannot win. [data-daylight] drops L1 and L2 entirely and swaps the tint for --plate at 0.94 α — same geometry, same type, same hues, no blur cost. It is a token flip, not a second design, and it doubles as the low-battery and prefers-reduced-transparency fallback.

The hex

Four things must land without a tap: who owns it, what special resource is on it, what is built on it, and whether it holds a Temple or the Anchor Stone. Everything else is second tier and may be quiet. Nothing here is a file — every hex is drawn from its own state.

Isometric library · one construction, many skins

Every object is a 2:1 isometric diamond plinth plus extrusions, all built from the same four-path recipe: top face at full colour, left face at 42% black, right face at 22% black, a hairline white top edge. Two hues per object, no gradients, no raster. A new building is a new set of extrusions, not a new asset.

{{ i.name }}
Bonus resources · the Civilization rule

A bonus resource is a thing standing on the ground, not a coloured dot. Wheat is a sheaf, fish is a fish, a gold vein is nuggets in the rock. Each sprite is drawn in its resource hue, so the colour law and the picture say the same thing twice — which is what lets it survive sunlight and a 6 mm map icon.

Placement never collides
The structure owns the hex centre; the resource stands at the lower-left, one third out. Both are bottom-anchored to the same ground plane, so a Granary and its wheat read as one farm rather than two stickers.
Below 14 px, the sprite becomes a badge
One breakpoint, not a redraw: the same symbol drops into a dark disc ringed in its resource hue and moves to the upper-right vertex. Detail disappears, hue and silhouette do not.
Unsurveyed ground shows a question
A cell you have not walked draws the badge outline in grey with no glyph. The map admits it does not know, instead of showing nothing and looking empty.

Moments
